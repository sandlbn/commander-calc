\chapter{Limits and Capacities}
\label{app:limits}

Every fixed limit in \ccprod{}, in one place. These are compiled into the
program; none of them can be raised without rebuilding it.

\section{The worksheet}

\begin{cctablethree}{Limit}{Value}{Reaching it gives}{2.0in}{0.95in}{1.85in}
Worksheets per workbook & 16 & \msg{Resource limit reached} \\
Columns per worksheet & 256 (\cellref{A}--\cellref{IV}) & \msg{Invalid cell
reference} \\
Rows per worksheet & 65,535 & \msg{Invalid cell reference} \\
\textbf{Cells per worksheet} & \textbf{16,384} & \msg{Resource limit reached} \\
Last addressable cell & \cellref{IV65535} & \\
Worksheet name & 31 characters & truncated \\
Worksheet name shown on a tab & 9 characters & truncated \\
\end{cctablethree}

\begin{ccnote}
The cell limit is per \emph{worksheet}, not per workbook. Sixteen worksheets of
16,384 cells each is 262,144 cells in one file --- if the machine's memory
stretches that far, which on a 512K machine it does not.
\end{ccnote}

\section{XLSX import}

\begin{cctablethree}{Limit}{Value}{Reaching it gives}{2.0in}{0.95in}{1.85in}
One archive part, expanded & 128K & \msg{Resource limit reached} \\
Shared strings mapped & 1,024 & \msg{(some did not fit)} \\
Formulas compiled per import & 512 & the rest keep cached values \\
Custom number formats & 32 & the rest read as General \\
One cell's raw value text & 512 bytes & truncated \\
Archive entry name & 48 characters & the part is not found \\
Worksheets & 16 & \msg{\emph{n} more sheets skipped} \\
\end{cctablethree}

\begin{ccnote}
The 128K part limit is the one that bites first, and it is about the
\emph{file}, not the worksheet. A worksheet's XML has to be held compressed and
expanded at the same time, with the growing cell store beside it. Measured: 500
days of six-column data (118K of XML) imported with 2M free; 2,000 days (478K)
was refused with 1.98M free. On data of that shape the ceiling is around three
thousand cells.
\end{ccnote}

\section{Memory}

\begin{cctable}{Item}{Size}{2.2in}{2.62in}
Banked RAM on a standard machine & 512K (64 banks of 8K) \\
Banked RAM, maximum & 2M (256 banks) \\
Available to the workbook & All of it except bank 0 \\
Largest single allocation & one bank, 8,192 bytes \\
A cell record & 8 bytes \\
Row index per worksheet & 1K, allocated once \\
Each 256-row band touched & 2K \\
Resident program & \$0801--\$7FFF \\

C stack & 512 bytes \\
\end{cctable}

Storage is sparse but banded. A worksheet using rows 1--100 costs 3K; a
worksheet with one cell in each of 256 scattered bands costs 513K. The
16,384-cell limit is what bounds that case.
