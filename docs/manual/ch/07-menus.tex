Six menus, and the division between them is by what a command acts on:

\begin{cctable}{Menu}{Holds}{1.1in}{3.72in}
\menupath{File} & Everything that touches the storage device. \\
\menupath{Edit} & The current cell and what is in it. \\
\menupath{Layout} & The shape of the sheet rather than its contents: which rows
and columns exist, how wide they are, and what stays on screen while the rest
scrolls. \\
\menupath{Data} & What is done \emph{to} the numbers: sorting them, and
recalculating them. \\
\menupath{Chart} & The three ways of drawing them. \\
\menupath{Sheet} & The worksheets themselves. \\
\end{cctable}

\section{Opening a menu}

Press \key{F10}, or click on a menu name.

\key{F10} opens \menupath{File}. From there \key{$\leftarrow$} and
\key{$\rightarrow$} move between the six menus, wrapping round at either end,
and \key{$\uparrow$} and \key{$\downarrow$} move the highlight through the
items, also wrapping. \key{Return} chooses the highlighted item. \key{Esc}
closes the menu without doing anything, and so does a second \key{F10}.

With the mouse: click a name on the bar to open it, slide along the bar to open
each in turn, move down the panel to highlight an item, and click to choose it.
Clicking anywhere off the menu closes it.

\begin{ccnote}
The highlight on the open menu's \emph{name} is four characters wide whatever
the name is. \menupath{Sheet} therefore appears as \msg{Shee} in reverse video
with a stray \msg{t} left in the bar colour beside it, and \menupath{Chart} as
\msg{Char} with a stray \msg{t}. It is cosmetic, and it is not a sign that
anything has gone wrong.
\end{ccnote}

\section{Function keys instead}

Every command on \menupath{File}, and seven of the eight on \menupath{Edit},
has a key printed beside it in the menu, and that key works from the worksheet
without opening the menu at all. Most of the time those two menus are there to
remind you what the keys are.

The other four menus have no keys at all. Sorting, recalculating, charting,
freezing, changing the shape of the grid and every worksheet command can only be
reached through the bar. \menupath{Replace...} is the one command on
\menupath{Edit} in the same position, sitting beside seven that do have keys.

\begin{ccnote}
The function keys also work \emph{while a menu is open}. Pressing \key{F7} with
the \menupath{Edit} menu on screen closes it and starts an import. Only
\key{PgUp} and \key{PgDn} are swallowed by an open menu.
\end{ccnote}

\section{What is not on the menus}

There is no \menupath{Help}, no \menupath{View} and no \menupath{Format} menu.
Formatting in particular has no home because there are no formatting commands to
put in one --- see Chapter~\ref{ch:formats}.

\begin{ccnote}
The bar has not always had six menus on it. Earlier versions advertised
\menupath{Cell}, \menupath{Sheet} and \menupath{Data} with nothing behind them,
then dropped back to three that worked; \menupath{Data} and \menupath{Chart}
returned when there was something to put in them, and \menupath{Layout} arrived
to take the rows, the columns and the widths off \menupath{Edit} --- which is
about editing again as a result. A menu appears here when it has commands, and
not before.
\end{ccnote}
